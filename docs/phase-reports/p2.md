## 1. What was built

Six scorers over a frozen trajectory schema, plus a dumb-pipe runner. The architectural bet —
the same one P1 made for the agent — is that **iteration and I/O live in the runner and all
judgment lives in pure functions**, so a scorer is independently testable and adding one is a
single registry line.

| Scorer | Question | Portable beyond this rubric? |
|---|---|---|
| pass^k | Same input k times — same answer? | yes, schema-generic |
| gate integrity | Is the escalation right, and is it right *for the stated reason*? | needs your gate vocabulary |
| agreement | How close to a human reference, per dimension and per divergence stratum? | needs your reference labels |
| ledger consistency | Does the agent contradict its own earlier findings later in the run? | needs your ledger structure |
| tool-call correctness | Structurally correct calls; does retry → degrade → escalate work? | yes, schema-generic |
| evidence coverage | Which conclusions rest on too little cited evidence? (**sentinel**, not a score) | yes, schema-generic |

Three contracts are enforced in code rather than convention, each because skipping one produced
a real wrong number earlier: **explicit batch scope** by run-id manifest (a directory scan once
mixed 114 stale runs into a batch, silently), **validation exclusion surfaced** in the report
header rather than swallowed, and **constructed variants never sharing a table** with observed
results.

## 3. The result that justifies the whole approach

Full section: [p2-cross-model.md](p2-cross-model.md). In brief:

`gpt-4o-mini` beats `deepseek-chat` on the number a conventional eval reports — **FN 6 → 0**, gate
stability **26/30 → 30/30**. Three trajectory-level measures disagree in the same direction:
trigger attribution **21/28 → 14/29**, `hard_requirements` degradations **1 → 20**, and the
delivery model **never once scores `hard_requirements` = 5** (distribution {0: 112, 3: 18} against
the dev model's {0: 125, 3: 5, 5: 19}).

The third fact explains the other two. A model that never judges a ledger fully met cannot clear
the veto, so it escalates everything; on a 29/30 gate-positive reference set that scores 29 TP and
1 FP. **The perfect stability is the stability of a constant function.**

This is [finding 004](../findings/004-gate-truth-imbalance.md)'s degenerate-positive-class warning
in concrete form: the confusion matrix cannot separate a better agent from a more indiscriminate
one. The trajectory metrics can, and did. **Reporting only the confusion matrix would have
recommended the model that reasons worse.**

## 4. Pre-registered predictions

Recorded in findings 011 and 013 *before* the delivery run, so the second model would be an
uncontaminated test rather than a post-hoc story. No semantic calibration preceded it — the one
permitted calibration round was spent entirely on request-shape compatibility.

**① "Education is the least self-consistent dimension." — DISCONFIRMED.** 7/30 stable pairs
(σ̄ 0.643) on the dev model becomes 23/30 (σ̄ 0.107) on the delivery model; `hard_requirements`
is now the least stable. **What was disconfirmed is the per-dimension ranking, which is
provider-specific and not a property of the rubric. What survives is finding 011's structural
claim — that a variance floor exists and bounds every improvement claim — which holds on both
models, in different shapes.** Those two sentences belong together; the ranking falling does not
take the finding with it.

**② "The veto is bistable, swinging 0↔5." — DISCONFIRMED, with a mechanism.** On the dev model it
held: 5 of 9 moving pairs swing 0↔5, confident opposites with nothing between. On the delivery
model there is no bistability *because one pole never occurs* — score 5 is absent from all 130
assessments and every moving pair moves 0↔3. Precise statement: **bistability is
provider-specific; the cross-provider commonality is that `hard_requirements` is unstable at its
decision boundary, and the shape of that instability differs by model** — flip versus hedge.

**③ "Faithfulness failures concentrate in `hard_requirements`." — NOT TESTED.** Finding 013's
result came from ten hand-judged samples; none were judged on the delivery model, so the
prediction stands untested rather than answered with the nearest available number. The
evidence-coverage sentinel did invert its concentration (`hard_requirements` 10 → 7 flags,
`skills_coverage` 2 → 15), recorded as **a provider difference in evidence distribution, not a
faithfulness conclusion** — the sentinel's own documentation forbids reading it as one.

## 5. Constructed variants

Two batches: gate negatives (to populate finding 004's empty true-negative class) and fault
samples (to exercise the retry → degrade → escalate path the live corpus barely touched).

**Fault batch: 5/5, verified at the fine-grained level and reproduced across two independent
batches.** Truncated documents and empty JDs fire the right anomaly rules; one injected malformed
response is recovered by a single retry with no degradation (`attempt 1 malformed_output →
attempt 2 ok`); two exhaust the retry, degrade the dimension, and raise `insufficient_evidence`.
The structural stage's error-recovery coverage caveat — that path fired on only 20/150 live runs —
**is resolved**.

**Gate negatives: 1–2 of 6 attempted, and the failure is the result.** Five of six constructions
diverged from their stated expectation. Diagnosed one at a time under the three-state rule
(construction error / agent–rubric divergence / undiagnosable), three were construction errors and
were **retired to `construction_failures` rather than counted**. The negative set is left
under-populated and declared as such: when the count gate and the correctness gate conflict,
**correctness wins** — padding it would manufacture phantom true negatives in the exact metric the
batch existed to repair.

## 7. Known limitations

Stated here rather than left for a reader to discover.

- **The constructed negative class is measured at k=1** while the system under test has a
  quantified variance floor. Negative-class results are **existence proofs** — "at least N inputs
  can produce this" — never rates. This is an internal inconsistency in the method: the variance
  floor was measured and then the constructed tests were run once each. The same variant flipped
  between two batches, which is how it was caught. Both batches are kept; neither was picked.
- **True negatives are scarce for three independent reasons**, all recorded: the low road is
  near-empty by score geometry (014), the high road must satisfy a ledger whose size is stochastic
  (015), and k=1 is underpowered. This is not a sampling accident and would not be fixed by
  drawing more pairs.
- **n = 30 pairs, one rubric, two models.** A fixed instrument for detecting change in one agent —
  not a benchmark, and not comparable across projects.
- **The faithfulness probe was a stratified sample of known weak spots**, 10 hand-judged cases
  drawn deliberately from the likeliest failures. **No overall faithfulness rate exists in this
  work and none can be derived from it.**
- **`hard_requirements` agreement is not comparable at face value across models** — the delivery
  model's 0.846 is computed over n=130 rather than 149 because 20 assessments degraded out.
- **Two known defects are frozen, not fixed** ([issue #26](https://github.com/xy9iao/trajectory-eval-harness/issues/26)):
  the extractor emits requirement categories the rubric excludes (012), and its ledger size is
  unstable (015). Both are single-variable calibrations deferred on purpose — the extractor feeds
  every hard-requirement verdict, so changing it invalidates every baseline recorded since P0.
